# Creature Naming Guidelines (Phonetic-Integration Standard)

> **Authoritative reference for naming all future creatures** — ransomware line, worm line, and any new lines.
> When a new creature name is needed, run it through this standard before proposing it.

---

## 1. The Core Constraint: Phonetic Transparency

The tech term must share a **phonetic sound** (vowel, consonant, or syllable) with the portion of the creature's name it is replacing. The parts that make up the word require a **phonetic bridge**. 

**The "Gliding" Rule:** If you replace a syllable, the transition between the animal name and the tech term must require *zero change* in mouth positioning. The portmanteau should feel like it was always one word — the tech term "glides" into the animal name without any audible break.

**Avoid "Forced" Blends:** If the resulting name requires a pause or sounds "choppy," it fails the standard. Say it out loud. If you hesitate mid-word, it doesn't pass.

---

## 2. Anti-Swallow Rule (Biological Anchoring)

The resulting portmanteau must **never be identical to the raw tech term**. The biological identity of the animal must survive the merge. Ensure the final word retains a *unique, animal-identifying syllable* that cannot be confused for a standard tech phrase.

**Bad example:** `Cryptography` — this is just the tech term. No animal identity survives.
**Good example:** `Cryptoad` — the "-toad" (amphibian) gives it away as an animal, and the "Crypt-" bridges to "Cryptography."

---

## 3. Standalone Base Rule (Suffix/Prefix Autonomy)

When attaching a tech term to a fragment of an animal's name, **that fragment must clearly evoke the animal on its own**. Avoid relying on implied adjectives (like "Golden") to make the animal identifiable.

Use animals with strong, singular, base-level names where the phonetic overlap bridges two **nouns**, not a noun and a verb.

**Bad example:** `Firewall` (the base "wall" is inanimate and not clearly an animal).
**Good example:** `FireWallaby` — "Wallaby" is the strong, clear animal base; the tech term replaces the initial "W" sound.

---

## 4. Verification Checklist (Must-Pass All Four)

Before proposing a new name, run it through every criterion. If any row returns **Fail**, revise the name.

| Criteria | Question to Ask | Pass / Fail |
|---|---|---|
| **Phonetic Overlap** | Does the tech term share at least one sound or syllable with the original animal word? | ☐ Yes / ☐ No |
| **Holistic Flow** | When said out loud, does it sound like a "natural" animal name — not a compound with a hyphen or pause? | ☐ Yes / ☐ No |
| **Thematic Integrity** | Does the tech term match the creature's lore role (e.g., defensive vs. malicious)? | ☐ Yes / ☐ No |
| **No Syllable Clash** | Did I remove clunky, overlapping sounds to ensure a smooth blend? | ☐ Yes / ☐ No |

---

## 5. Approved Examples (Benchmarks)

Use these as the **stylistic baseline** for future creature designs. Note how each one passes all four checklist items.

| Name | Base Animal | Tech Term | What Bridges It |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wallabyte** | Wallaby | Byte | The "by/byte" sound glides in naturally. |
| **Koalauth** | Koala | Auth | Perfect blending on the "auth" syllable. |
| **Kangaroot** | Kangaroo | Root | Replaces the "oo" vowel with the "oo" in *root*. |
| **Belugate** | Beluga | Gate | The "ga" sound bridges both words. |
| **Marspoof** | Marsupial | Spoof | Clean consonant swap on the final structure. |
| **Booleen** | Baleen | Boolean | Seamless shift from the "leen" sound. |
| **Bandicable** | Bandicoot | Cable | Natural-sounding blending coming off of the letter 'c'. |

---

## 6. Anti-Patterns (What to Avoid)

| Anti-Pattern | Example | Why It Fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Stop-Start Portmanteau** | "Firewall-Marsupial" | Too long, no phonetic blending. Just two words jammed together. |
| **Forced Phonetic Shift** | "Wombatcode" | The "t-c" consonant cluster is clunky and breaks the flow. |
| **Semantic Clutter** | Any name where the pun requires explanation | If you have to explain the pun, it is too complex. The animal must read at a glance. |
| **Tech-Swallow** | "Ransom" (standalone) | The raw tech term with no animal identity surviving. Fails §2. |

---

## 7. Application Scope

This standard applies to:

- **All creature names** across all lines (Ransomware, Worm, and any future lines).
- **All lifecycle stages** (Boot Sector, Process, Script, Daemon).
- **Both evolution branches** (Good / Bad) — naming quality is independent of branch.
