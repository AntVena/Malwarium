HOME FLASH DEMO

A demo, so you know what you'd be buying.

Malwarium is a handheld — a little ESP32 gadget you keep in a pocket and raise a tamed computer virus on, over days. This page is not that. It is the device's own firmware compiled to run in a browser, handed a pet somebody else already raised, so you can poke the systems for ten minutes and see whether they're for you.

Everything below the screen is the honest version of what that trade costs.

WHAT THE DEMO ISN'T

YOUR PROGRESS HERE IS NOT SAFE, AND THAT IS ON PURPOSE Whatever you do on this page is kept in this browser, on this machine, and nowhere else. Clearing site data loses it. A private window never had it. Another device never sees it.

More than that: the save format moves with the firmware, and old formats get retired once every real device has moved past them — a check that can be made for devices and cannot be made for anonymous browser tabs. So an update here can quietly reset you to the starting pet, with no warning and no way back. On a real device that migration is handled and your pet survives. Do not get attached to anything in this tab.
THE SLOW PART IS THE ACTUAL PRODUCT A real device starts you with an encrypted egg and a ~30-minute incubation, and from there the pet is raised over days — it gets hungry while you're not looking, and what it grows into depends on how you treated it. That is the whole point of the thing, and it is exactly what a ten-minute browser session cannot show you. So this demo skips it: you get a pet already six levels in, with things to spend, because the alternative is watching an egg.
HALF THE DEVICE IS MISSING HERE CFG, SCAN, LINK and PEERS are dimmed on the shelf. They are the radio: joining Wi-Fi, sniffing the air for networks to tame and feed to the pet, linking to another Malwarium to duel it, installing its own updates. A browser has no radio, so they stay visible-but-shut rather than opening onto a screen that could only say nothing found. The hardware half of Malwarium is not on this page.

WHAT IS REAL ABOUT IT

The game itself. This is the device's own firmware — the same C++ engine, the same 240×240 panel, the same three buttons — compiled to WebAssembly and drawn onto a canvas. Nothing is re-implemented for the web, so the fights, the arcade, the items and the art are exactly what the hardware runs. What you think of the systems here is a fair basis for what you'd think of the device.
